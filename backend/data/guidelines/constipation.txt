Constipation - Triage Guidelines

OVERVIEW
Constipation is infrequent or difficult bowel movements. Most cases are mild and resolve with dietary and lifestyle changes. Severe cases may indicate obstruction.

MILD CONSTIPATION (Home Care)
- Symptoms:
  * Fewer bowel movements than usual (every 2-3 days instead of daily)
  * Hard, dry stools
  * Mild straining or difficulty passing stool
  * Mild abdominal bloating or discomfort
  * No pain
  * No blood in stool
  * Recent dietary change, travel, or reduced activity
  * Started after medication or dietary supplement

- Recommended actions:
  * Increase fiber intake gradually (fruits, vegetables, whole grains, legumes)
  * Drink plenty of water (6-8 glasses daily)
  * Increase physical activity and exercise
  * Don't ignore urge to pass stool
  * Use toilet regularly, especially after meals
  * Eat prunes, dates, or figs for natural laxative effect
  * Consider fiber supplement (psyllium husk) if dietary changes insufficient
  * Avoid straining excessively
  * Over-the-counter osmotic laxatives may help (MiraLAX, lactulose)
  * Avoid stimulant laxatives for prolonged use
  * Most cases resolve in a few days with lifestyle changes

MODERATE CONSTIPATION (Visit PHC within 24-48 hours)
- Symptoms:
  * No bowel movement for >3 days despite home measures
  * Hard, difficult stools with significant straining
  * Moderate abdominal bloating or discomfort
  * Pain during bowel movement
  * Feeling of incomplete evacuation
  * Mild abdominal pain not resolving
  * Straining causing external hemorrhoids or anal fissures
  * Constipation following surgery or major medication change
  * Associated with weight changes or significant dietary changes
  * Constipation alternating with diarrhea

- Recommended actions:
  * Continue high-fiber diet and water intake
  * Use over-the-counter stool softeners (docusate)
  * Increase physical activity
  * Seek medical evaluation if symptoms persist >5-7 days
  * Seek care if pain or rectal bleeding develops
  * Medical evaluation to identify underlying cause
  * Consider medication side effects with doctor

SEVERE CONSTIPATION (Seek immediate medical attention)
- Symptoms:
  * Complete inability to pass stool for >5 days
  * Severe abdominal pain or distention
  * Severe bloating affecting breathing or eating
  * Nausea or vomiting with constipation
  * Abdominal rigidity or severe tenderness
  * Bloody stools or significant rectal bleeding
  * Severe straining causing dizziness or fainting
  * Fever with severe constipation
  * Signs of fecal impaction (liquid stool leakage around blockage, inability to pass solid stool)
  * Recent abdominal surgery or trauma

- Emergency red flags:
  * Signs of bowel obstruction (severe pain, vomiting, distention)
  * Signs of peritonitis (severe abdominal pain, rigid abdomen, fever)
  * Complete cessation of bowel movements with severe nausea/vomiting
  * Suspected fecal impaction causing complications

COMMON CAUSES
- Low-fiber diet
- Inadequate fluid intake
- Sedentary lifestyle
- Medication side effects (opioids, some antacids, antihistamines)
- Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)
- Thyroid problems or metabolic disorders
- Pregnancy
- Anal fissures or hemorrhoids causing pain-related avoidance
- Bowel obstruction (emergency)
- Neurological disorders

PREVENTION
- Maintain high-fiber diet (25-30g daily)
- Drink 6-8 glasses of water daily
- Regular physical activity and exercise
- Establish regular bowel routine (same time daily)
- Don't delay when feeling urge to defecate
- Manage stress
- Review medications with doctor

HIGH-RISK POPULATIONS
- Elderly individuals
- Pregnant women
- Post-surgery patients
- Bedridden individuals
- On opioid medications

WHEN TO SEEK CARE
- Constipation lasting >1 week despite home measures
- Severe abdominal pain with constipation
- Bloody stools or significant rectal bleeding
- Severe straining or pain during bowel movement
- Persistent symptoms with weight loss
- Age >50 with new onset constipation
- Vomiting with constipation (possible obstruction)
