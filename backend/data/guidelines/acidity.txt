Acidity (Acid Reflux/GERD) - Triage Guidelines

OVERVIEW
Acidity results from stomach acid backing up into the esophagus. Range from occasional heartburn (home care) to chronic GERD requiring medical management.

MILD ACIDITY - OCCASIONAL HEARTBURN (Home Care)
- Symptoms:
  * Occasional heartburn (1-2 times per week)
  * Burning sensation in chest or throat
  * Worse after eating certain foods (spicy, fatty, acidic)
  * Better with standing or walking
  * No chest pain (true cardiac pain)
  * No difficulty swallowing
  * No weight loss
  * Duration: relief within minutes to hours

- Recommended actions:
  * Avoid trigger foods (citrus, tomato, chocolate, coffee, spicy foods)
  * Avoid large meals; eat smaller frequent meals
  * Don't eat before bedtime (3+ hours before sleep)
  * Elevate head while sleeping (use extra pillow)
  * Avoid tight clothing that compresses abdomen
  * Drink water to dilute stomach acid
  * Chew gum to stimulate saliva (neutralizes acid)
  * Antacids (calcium carbonate, magnesium hydroxide) can provide relief
  * Weight management if overweight
  * Avoid alcohol and smoking
  * Stress management

MODERATE ACIDITY (Visit PHC within 1-2 weeks)
- Symptoms:
  * Heartburn 3+ times per week
  * Burning sensation in chest or upper abdomen
  * Mild regurgitation of food or acid
  * Difficulty sleeping due to reflux
  * Sore throat from acid exposure
  * Mild cough or hoarseness
  * Symptoms lasting >3 months
  * Despite antacids, symptoms persist
  * Waking at night due to heartburn

- Recommended actions:
  * Follow all preventive measures above
  * Use antacids regularly before meals and at bedtime
  * Consider H2 receptor blockers (famotidine, ranitidine)
  * Provide detailed history to healthcare provider
  * May need prescription-strength acid reducer
  * Identify and avoid all trigger foods
  * Lifestyle modification is essential
  * Weight loss if overweight
  * Manage stress through relaxation techniques

SEVERE ACIDITY / GERD (Seek medical care)
- Symptoms:
  * Daily or near-daily heartburn affecting quality of life
  * Severe burning pain in chest or throat
  * Significant regurgitation making it difficult to eat or sleep
  * Difficulty swallowing (dysphagia)
  * Sensation of food getting stuck
  * Chronic cough or wheezing
  * Persistent hoarseness or voice changes
  * Nocturnal reflux waking multiple times
  * Weight loss or nutritional decline
  * Symptoms despite antacids and lifestyle changes

ATYPICAL GERD (May mimic other conditions - seek evaluation)
- Symptoms:
  * Chest pain that may mimic cardiac pain
  * Difficulty swallowing
  * Chronic cough without cold
  * Asthma-like symptoms (wheezing, shortness of breath)
  * Hoarseness or loss of voice
  * Chronic sore throat
  * Bad breath

EMERGENCY SIGNS (Seek immediate care if occurring with reflux):
- Symptoms:
  * Severe chest pain (could be cardiac)
  * Difficulty swallowing with vomiting
  * Vomiting blood or material resembling coffee grounds
  * Severe difficulty breathing
  * Signs of heart attack (chest pain, shortness of breath, arm/jaw pain)

RISK FACTORS FOR SEVERE GERD
- Obesity or overweight
- Pregnancy
- Smoking
- Alcohol consumption
- Chronic stress
- Certain medications (NSAIDs, aspirin, calcium channel blockers, nitrates)
- History of hiatal hernia

COMMON TRIGGER FOODS
- Spicy foods
- Citrus fruits (orange, lemon, grapefruit)
- Tomatoes and tomato products
- Chocolate
- Coffee and caffeinated drinks
- Alcohol, especially wine and beer
- Fatty or fried foods
- Mint (may relax esophageal sphincter)
- Garlic and onions
- Carbonated beverages

MANAGEMENT ESCALATION
- Mild: Dietary changes, antacids PRN
- Moderate: Regular antacids, H2 blockers, lifestyle modification
- Severe: Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs), specialist evaluation

WHEN TO SEEK CARE
- Heartburn occurring >3 times per week
- Symptoms not relieved by antacids
- Difficulty swallowing
- Regurgitation of blood
- Chronic cough or hoarseness
- Chest pain (especially if also cardiac risk factors)
- Weight loss with reflux
- Symptoms affecting sleep and daily life
