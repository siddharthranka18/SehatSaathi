Gastritis - Triage Guidelines

OVERVIEW
Gastritis is inflammation of the stomach lining. Symptoms range from mild discomfort to severe pain. Most cases are self-limiting but require monitoring.

MILD GASTRITIS (Home Care)
- Symptoms:
  * Burning or mild ache in upper abdomen
  * Mild nausea (no vomiting)
  * Loss of appetite
  * Feeling uncomfortably full after eating small amounts
  * No fever
  * Duration: 1-2 days
  * Triggered by spicy food, alcohol, or NSAIDs

- Recommended actions:
  * Avoid spicy, acidic, hot, greasy foods
  * Eat bland foods (rice, plain bread, boiled vegetables, chicken broth)
  * Eat small frequent meals instead of large meals
  * Drink milk or eat yogurt to soothe stomach
  * Avoid coffee, tea, and carbonated drinks
  * Rest and reduce stress
  * Stop NSAIDs (aspirin, ibuprofen) if possible
  * Drink plenty of water

MODERATE GASTRITIS (Visit PHC within 24-48 hours)
- Symptoms:
  * Burning or dull ache in upper abdomen persisting >24 hours
  * Mild vomiting 1-2 times
  * Nausea interfering with eating
  * Mild heartburn or acid reflux
  * Black or tarry stools (sign of bleeding)
  * Feeling full early or bloating
  * Mild fever (99-100.5°F / 37.2-38°C)
  * Symptoms not improving after 24 hours of home care

- Recommended actions:
  * Seek medical evaluation if symptoms persist >24 hours
  * Avoid all triggering foods and alcohol
  * Do NOT take NSAIDs
  * Use antacids or take prescribed acid-reducing medication
  * Follow strict bland diet
  * Manage stress through rest

SEVERE GASTRITIS (Seek immediate medical attention)
- Symptoms:
  * Severe burning pain in upper abdomen
  * Persistent vomiting (unable to keep food/water down)
  * Vomit containing blood or blood clots
  * Black, tarry stools or visible blood in stool
  * Severe weakness or dizziness
  * Fever >101°F (>38.3°C) with severe symptoms
  * Chest pain or difficulty swallowing
  * Signs of dehydration (dry mouth, dark urine, extreme thirst)

- Emergency red flags:
  * Hematemesis (coughing up blood)
  * Severe anemia symptoms (extreme fatigue, shortness of breath, pale skin)
  * Signs of peritonitis (rigid abdomen, severe tenderness)
  * Severe dehydration

COMMON TRIGGERS
- NSAIDs (aspirin, ibuprofen, naproxen)
- Spicy foods
- Alcohol
- Coffee and caffeinated drinks
- Smoking
- Stress and anxiety
- H. pylori infection
- Bile reflux

FOLLOW-UP CARE
- If diagnosed with H. pylori: complete full course of antibiotics
- After acute phase: continue bland diet until symptoms fully resolve
- Preventive measures: avoid triggers, manage stress, take medications as prescribed
