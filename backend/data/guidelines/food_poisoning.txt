Food Poisoning - Triage Guidelines

OVERVIEW
Food poisoning results from eating contaminated food. Onset varies from 1 hour to 72 hours after ingestion. Most cases are self-limiting but require hydration and monitoring.

MILD FOOD POISONING (Home Care)
- Symptoms:
  * Mild nausea or vomiting 1-2 times
  * Mild diarrhea (1-3 episodes)
  * Mild stomach cramps
  * No fever or low-grade fever (<99.5°F / <37.5°C)
  * Able to drink fluids
  * Symptoms onset 1-24 hours after eating
  * Normal energy level

- Recommended actions:
  * Rest and avoid food for first few hours
  * Start sipping oral rehydration solution (ORS) or water
  * Once nausea subsides, eat bland foods (rice, crackers, banana, boiled vegetables)
  * Avoid dairy, spicy, fatty, and greasy foods
  * Avoid alcohol
  * Continue fluids frequently in small amounts
  * Symptoms usually resolve in 24-48 hours
  * Monitor for worsening signs

MODERATE FOOD POISONING (Visit PHC within 12-24 hours)
- Symptoms:
  * Persistent vomiting (4+ episodes in 24 hours)
  * Moderate diarrhea (4-6 episodes daily)
  * Moderate stomach cramps and pain
  * Fever 99.5-101°F (37.5-38.3°C)
  * Signs of mild dehydration (thirst, dark urine, dry mouth)
  * Unable to keep fluids down initially
  * Symptoms lasting >24 hours without improvement
  * Diarrhea with visible blood or mucus
  * Multiple people affected (suggests food source)

- Recommended actions:
  * Seek medical evaluation if dehydration develops or symptoms worsen
  * Continue ORS in frequent small amounts
  * Once vomiting subsides, start bland foods
  * Monitor for signs of dehydration
  * Avoid antibiotics unless specific bacteria identified
  * Rest completely
  * Seek care if unable to keep fluids down for 6+ hours

SEVERE FOOD POISONING (Seek immediate medical attention)
- Symptoms:
  * Severe, persistent vomiting (unable to retain any fluids)
  * Severe diarrhea (>10 episodes daily or bloody)
  * Severe abdominal pain and cramping
  * Fever >101°F (>38.3°C)
  * Signs of severe dehydration:
    - Extreme thirst, very dry mouth
    - Little or no urine output
    - Extreme weakness or dizziness
    - Confusion or altered mental status
  * Blood in vomit or stool
  * Signs of shock (pale, cold, rapid heartbeat)
  * Persistent pain after vomiting stops
  * Symptoms in very young children, elderly, or immunocompromised individuals
  * Foodborne botulism signs (blurred vision, difficulty swallowing, paralysis)

- Emergency red flags:
  * Unable to drink fluids for >6 hours with active vomiting
  * Severe dehydration with signs of shock
  * Severe bloody diarrhea
  * High fever with severe symptoms
  * Signs of systemic infection

PREVENTION
- Proper food storage and hygiene
- Cook foods to safe temperatures
- Avoid raw or undercooked eggs, meat, seafood
- Wash hands before eating
- Use clean utensils and cutting boards
- Don't leave cooked food at room temperature >2 hours

RECOVERY TIMELINE
- Mild cases: 24-48 hours
- Moderate cases: 2-5 days
- Severe cases: may require hospitalization and IV fluids

WHEN TO SEEK CARE
- Symptoms lasting >3 days
- Signs of dehydration
- Blood in stool or vomit
- Severe pain
- Fever >101°F (>38.3°C)
- Symptoms in vulnerable populations (infant, elderly, pregnant)
