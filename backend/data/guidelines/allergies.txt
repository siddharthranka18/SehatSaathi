Allergies - Triage Guidelines

OVERVIEW
Allergic reactions can range from mild local irritation to life-threatening anaphylaxis. Triage depends on exposure, spread, and breathing/throat involvement.

MILD ALLERGY (Home Care)
- Symptoms:
  * Sneezing, runny nose, itchy eyes
  * Mild localized rash or itching
  * Mild swelling limited to one area
  * No breathing difficulty
  * No throat swelling
  * No dizziness or fainting
  * Symptoms appear after a known trigger

- Recommended actions:
  * Remove/avoid the trigger if possible
  * Oral antihistamine (if appropriate)
  * Cool compress for itchy skin
  * Wash exposed skin after contact
  * Monitor for worsening or spreading symptoms

MODERATE ALLERGY (Visit PHC within 24 hours)
- Symptoms:
  * Widespread itching or hives
  * Facial swelling but no breathing compromise
  * Recurrent allergic episodes
  * Mild throat discomfort without swelling
  * Symptoms not improving with antihistamines
  * Possible food, medication, or insect trigger needing identification

- Recommended actions:
  * Stop exposure to suspected trigger
  * Seek outpatient evaluation
  * Document timing and trigger
  * Continue antihistamine if advised

SEVERE ALLERGY / ANAPHYLAXIS (Immediate emergency care)
- Symptoms:
  * Difficulty breathing, wheezing, or shortness of breath
  * Throat swelling or trouble swallowing
  * Severe facial or tongue swelling
  * Dizziness, fainting, confusion
  * Chest tightness or collapse
  * Rapid worsening after exposure

- Emergency actions:
  * Use epinephrine if prescribed
  * Call emergency services immediately
  * Do not delay for home treatment

COMMON TRIGGERS
- Foods: peanuts, tree nuts, shellfish, eggs, milk, wheat, soy
- Medications: penicillin, NSAIDs, some antibiotics
- Insect stings
- Pollens, dust, mold, pet dander
- Latex, fragrances, skincare products

WHEN TO SEEK CARE
- First allergic reaction with uncertain cause
- Symptoms spreading beyond one area
- Any throat or breathing symptom
- Recurrent hives or swelling
- Medication allergy symptoms
