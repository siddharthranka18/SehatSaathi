Diarrhea - Triage Guidelines

OVERVIEW
Diarrhea is frequent loose or watery stool. Most cases are acute and self-limiting lasting 1-7 days. Severe cases with dehydration risk require urgent care.

MILD DIARRHEA (Home Care)
- Symptoms:
  * Loose or watery stools 2-3 times per day
  * Duration <24 hours
  * No fever or low-grade fever (<99.5°F / <37.5°C)
  * No blood or mucus in stool
  * Mild abdominal cramping or discomfort
  * Able to eat and drink normally
  * Normal energy level
  * No signs of dehydration

- Recommended actions:
  * Drink plenty of fluids (water, coconut water, ORS)
  * Eat bland foods (rice, banana, crackers, boiled vegetables, white bread)
  * Avoid dairy, spicy, fatty, and greasy foods
  * Avoid high-fiber foods temporarily
  * Rest and avoid strenuous activity
  * Wash hands frequently to prevent spread
  * Most cases resolve naturally in 24-48 hours
  * Over-the-counter rehydration salts can help

MODERATE DIARRHEA (Visit PHC within 24 hours)
- Symptoms:
  * Frequent loose stools (4-8 per day)
  * Duration 24-48 hours without improvement
  * Mild to moderate abdominal cramping
  * Mild fever (99.5-101°F / 37.5-38.3°C)
  * Signs of mild dehydration (thirst, slightly dark urine)
  * Diarrhea with visible mucus
  * Associated with mild nausea or vomiting
  * Recent food or water source change or travel
  * After antibiotic use (possible C.difficile)

- Recommended actions:
  * Aggressive rehydration with ORS or electrolyte solution
  * Drink small frequent amounts rather than large volumes
  * Eat bland foods once tolerated
  * Avoid dairy except plain yogurt
  * Note food triggers for future reference
  * Seek care if unable to keep fluids down
  * Seek care if duration >3 days or symptoms worsen
  * Antidiarrheal medications should be used cautiously

SEVERE DIARRHEA (Seek immediate medical attention)
- Symptoms:
  * Severe, frequent diarrhea (>8 episodes daily)
  * Persistent diarrhea lasting >4 days
  * Bloody diarrhea or diarrhea with significant mucus
  * Severe abdominal pain or cramping
  * Fever >101°F (>38.3°C) with diarrhea
  * Signs of severe dehydration:
    - Extreme thirst, very dry mouth
    - Very dark or no urine output
    - Dizziness or fainting
    - Confusion or lethargy
    - Pale or cold skin
  * Unable to drink fluids due to vomiting
  * Severe dehydration with weight loss (>5%)
  * Persistent abdominal pain

- Emergency red flags:
  * Signs of shock (extreme weakness, rapid heartbeat, confusion)
  * Severe bloody diarrhea
  * Unable to drink for 6+ hours with active diarrhea
  * Diarrhea with high fever and severe abdominal pain
  * Suspected sepsis or severe infection

HIGH-RISK GROUPS REQUIRING FASTER CARE
- Infants and young children (<5 years)
- Elderly (>65 years)
- Pregnant women
- Immunocompromised individuals
- Pre-existing kidney or heart disease

COMMON CAUSES
- Viral gastroenteritis (rotavirus, norovirus, adenovirus)
- Bacterial diarrhea (Salmonella, Shigella, E.coli, Campylobacter)
- Parasitic infection (Giardia, Cryptosporidium)
- Food poisoning
- Lactose intolerance
- Dietary changes or high-fat foods
- Antibiotic side effects
- IBS or inflammatory bowel disease
- Celiac disease or gluten sensitivity
- Medication side effects

MANAGEMENT
- Hydration is most critical (ORS, water, electrolyte drinks)
- Bland diet (BRAT diet: banana, rice, applesauce, toast)
- Rest and limit activity
- Avoid dairy, fatty, spicy foods
- Antibiotics only if bacterial pathogen identified
- Probiotics may help restore gut flora

WHEN TO SEEK CARE
- Duration >3 days, especially if worsening
- Blood or mucus in stool
- High fever with diarrhea
- Severe abdominal pain
- Signs of dehydration
- Severe diarrhea (>8 times daily)
- In infants, young children, elderly
- During pregnancy
- Recent antibiotic use with severe symptoms
