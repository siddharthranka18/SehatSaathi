Nausea and Vomiting - Triage Guidelines

OVERVIEW
Nausea and vomiting are symptoms of many conditions ranging from minor to serious. Triage depends on severity, duration, associated symptoms, and patient age.

MILD NAUSEA/VOMITING (Home Care)
- Symptoms:
  * Mild nausea without vomiting or vomiting once
  * Mild nausea triggered by specific triggers (smells, foods, motion)
  * Able to drink fluids without difficulty
  * No fever
  * Normal energy level
  * No abdominal pain or mild discomfort only
  * Duration <2 hours or episodic

- Recommended actions:
  * Rest in a quiet, cool environment
  * Avoid strong smells and visual triggers
  * Sip small amounts of water or clear fluids
  * Eat light foods once nausea subsides (crackers, toast)
  * Ginger tea or mint tea may help
  * Avoid dairy, spicy, fatty foods
  * Fresh air and slow deep breathing
  * Monitor for worsening

MODERATE NAUSEA/VOMITING (Visit PHC within 24 hours)
- Symptoms:
  * Vomiting 2-4 times in a few hours
  * Persistent nausea lasting >2 hours
  * Unable to keep solid foods down but can tolerate liquids
  * Mild to moderate abdominal pain or cramping
  * Low-grade fever (99-100.5°F / 37.2-38°C)
  * Mild dehydration signs (thirst, slightly dark urine)
  * Symptoms following food, medication, or travel
  * Associated with diarrhea (mild, <3 episodes)
  * Duration 4-12 hours without improvement

- Recommended actions:
  * Rest completely
  * Rehydrate with oral rehydration solution (ORS) in small frequent doses
  * Avoid food until vomiting subsides
  * Start bland foods once stable (rice, banana, crackers)
  * Avoid triggers (smells, movement, stressful activities)
  * Stay cool and get fresh air
  * Seek care if unable to keep fluids down or symptoms worsen
  * If medication-related: do not restart without physician guidance

SEVERE NAUSEA/VOMITING (Seek immediate medical attention)
- Symptoms:
  * Persistent vomiting (multiple times per hour, unable to keep any fluids down)
  * Severe abdominal pain with vomiting
  * Vomit containing blood or resembling coffee grounds
  * High fever (>101°F / >38.3°C) with severe vomiting
  * Signs of severe dehydration:
    - Very dark or no urine output
    - Extreme weakness, dizziness, or fainting
    - Dry mouth and lips, extreme thirst
    - Confusion or altered consciousness
  * Severe headache with stiff neck
  * Abdominal distention with severe pain
  * Persistent vomiting >4 hours without relief
  * Vomiting after head injury or recent trauma
  * Green/bilious vomit (suggests bowel obstruction)
  * Suspected poisoning or medication overdose

- Emergency red flags:
  * Unable to drink for >6 hours with active vomiting
  * Signs of shock (pale, cold, rapid heartbeat)
  * Severe dehydration with altered mental status
  * Blood in vomit
  * Severe abdominal pain with rigid abdomen
  * Vomiting with severe headache and stiff neck (meningitis)

COMMON CAUSES
- Gastroenteritis (food poisoning, viral, bacterial)
- Motion sickness
- Medication side effects
- Migraine or severe headache
- Pregnancy (first trimester)
- Anxiety or stress
- Chemotherapy or radiation
- Alcohol intoxication
- Appendicitis or bowel obstruction
- Meningitis or other infections
- Concussion or head injury

MANAGEMENT BY CAUSE
- Gastroenteritis: ORS, bland diet, antiemetics if prescribed
- Medication side effect: talk to doctor; may need adjustment
- Migraine: rest, dark quiet room, prescribed pain medication
- Motion sickness: fix gaze on horizon, ginger, antihistamines
- Pregnancy: small frequent meals, ginger, vitamin B6

WHEN TO SEEK CARE
- Vomiting lasting >6 hours
- Signs of dehydration
- Blood in vomit
- Severe abdominal pain
- High fever with vomiting
- Vomiting after head injury
- Suspected poisoning
- In infants, young children, elderly, or pregnancy
