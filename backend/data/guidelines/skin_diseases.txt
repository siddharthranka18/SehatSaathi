Skin Diseases - Triage Guidelines

OVERVIEW
Skin conditions range from minor irritations to serious infections requiring urgent care. Triage depends on extent, severity, symptoms, and spread.

MILD SKIN CONDITIONS (Home Care)
- Symptoms:
  * Localized rash affecting <10% of body
  * No fever
  * Non-itchy or mildly itchy
  * No pain or mild discomfort
  * No signs of infection (no pus, warmth, spreading redness)
  * Rash present 1-2 days
  * Clear trigger (new soap, detergent, plant contact)

- Examples:
  * Mild diaper rash
  * Heat rash
  * Mild contact dermatitis from poison ivy/oak
  * Minor insect bites
  * Mild seborrheic dermatitis
  * Small areas of eczema

- Recommended actions:
  * Stop contact with trigger (harsh soaps, irritants)
  * Keep area clean and dry
  * Wear soft, breathable clothing
  * Avoid scratching
  * Use unscented moisturizer
  * Over-the-counter hydrocortisone cream for mild inflammation
  * Calamine lotion or antihistamine for itching
  * Oatmeal baths for itch relief
  * Monitor for progression

MODERATE SKIN CONDITIONS (Visit PHC within 24-48 hours)
- Symptoms:
  * Rash affecting 10-30% of body
  * Significant itching causing difficulty sleeping
  * Mild fever (99-100.5°F / 37.2-38°C)
  * Pustules or mild signs of infection (slight warmth, minimal pus)
  * Rash present 3-7 days
  * Rash not improving with home care
  * Rash in sensitive areas (face, genitals, between toes)
  * Painful blisters or erosions
  * Mild swelling of affected area
  * Difficulty with daily activities due to itching/pain

- Examples:
  * Moderate contact dermatitis
  * Moderate eczema flare
  * Fungal infection (ringworm, athlete's foot) starting to spread
  * Moderate acne with inflammation
  * Shingles (varicella-zoster) affecting limited area
  * Impetigo (small areas, limited spread)

- Recommended actions:
  * Keep area clean and dry
  * Avoid triggers
  * Don't share towels, clothing, or personal items
  * Use prescribed antifungal or antibiotic cream
  * Take prescribed antihistamine for itch
  * Wear loose, soft clothing
  * Avoid harsh soaps
  * Seek care if worsening or new lesions appear

SEVERE SKIN CONDITIONS (Seek immediate medical attention)
- Symptoms:
  * Rash affecting >30% of body or rapid spread
  * High fever (>101°F / >38.3°C) with rash
  * Signs of serious infection:
    - Warmth, increasing redness, pus-filled lesions
    - Red streaking from the lesion (lymphangitis)
    - Swollen lymph nodes
  * Severe pain or tenderness
  * Difficulty moving affected areas
  * Facial edema or involvement around eyes
  * Severe blistering with erosions and weeping
  * Facial rash with fever (possible meningitis)
  * Rash with systemic symptoms (joint pain, fatigue, respiratory symptoms)
  * Petechial rash (small red dots that don't blanch) - possible meningitis
  * Signs of sepsis (fever, severe weakness, confusion)

- Emergency red flags:
  * Purple/dark rash that doesn't blanch (possible meningococcemia)
  * Severe facial swelling
  * Difficulty breathing or swallowing
  * Severe systemic illness with rash
  * Immunocompromised with spreading rash/infection

COMMON CONDITIONS BY PRESENTATION

Itchy Rash:
- Contact dermatitis
- Eczema
- Urticaria (hives)
- Pruritus
- Heat rash

Painful Rash:
- Shingles (dermatomal distribution)
- Burns
- Impetigo
- Erysipelas

Scaling Rash:
- Fungal infection (ringworm, athlete's foot)
- Psoriasis
- Seborrheic dermatitis

Pustular Rash:
- Acne
- Impetigo (superinfected wounds)
- Folliculitis

Blistering Rash:
- Chickenpox (varicella)
- Shingles (herpes zoster)
- Bullous pemphigoid
- Pemphigus

PREVENTION
- Keep skin clean and dry
- Moisturize regularly
- Avoid known triggers and irritants
- Use high-SPF sunscreen
- Avoid sharing personal items
- Maintain good hygiene
- Don't scratch or pick at lesions
- Wear protective clothing for outdoor activities

DANGER SIGNS REQUIRING IMMEDIATE CARE
- Petechial or purpuric rash (doesn't blanch)
- Fever with widespread rash
- Blistering with severe pain
- Facial rash with swelling
- Signs of infection with spreading warmth/redness
- Rash with difficulty breathing or swallowing
- New rash in immunocompromised individual

WHEN TO SEEK CARE
- Rash spreading rapidly or affecting >20% of body
- Fever with rash
- Severe pain or itching
- Signs of infection
- Rash lasting >1 week without improvement
- Rash with systemic symptoms
- Facial involvement
- Blistering or erosions
- Rash not responding to home care
