Fungal Infection - Triage Guidelines

OVERVIEW
Fungal infections commonly affect skin, feet, groin, nails, and mouth. They are often itchy, scaly, or white in appearance and may spread slowly.

MILD FUNGAL INFECTION (Home Care)
- Symptoms:
  * Localized ring-shaped rash or scaling
  * Itching with clear borders
  * Athlete's foot between toes
  * Groin rash without fever
  * Small area of skin involvement
  * No pain or only mild discomfort
  * No signs of bacterial infection

- Recommended actions:
  * Keep area clean and dry
  * Use topical antifungal cream as directed
  * Change socks/clothes daily
  * Avoid sharing towels or footwear
  * Wash hands after touching affected area
  * Continue treatment for the full recommended course

MODERATE FUNGAL INFECTION (Visit PHC within 24-48 hours)
- Symptoms:
  * Rash spreading slowly despite home care
  * Nail infection
  * Oral thrush
  * Genital fungal infection
  * Itching severe enough to disrupt sleep
  * Recurrent infection
  * Symptoms lasting >2 weeks

- Recommended actions:
  * Seek evaluation for stronger topical or oral treatment
  * Check for diabetes or immune issues if recurrent
  * Avoid using steroid creams alone

SEVERE FUNGAL INFECTION (Seek immediate medical attention)
- Symptoms:
  * Fever with spreading skin infection
  * Rapid spread with pain, warmth, or pus
  * Facial involvement around eyes or mouth
  * Oral thrush with trouble swallowing
  * Immunocompromised patient with worsening infection

- Emergency red flags:
  * Breathing difficulty
  * Severe swelling of face or throat
  * Signs of systemic infection

COMMON TYPES
- Ringworm (tinea corporis)
- Athlete's foot (tinea pedis)
- Jock itch (tinea cruris)
- Oral thrush (candidiasis)
- Nail fungus (onychomycosis)

PREVENTION
- Keep skin dry and clean
- Change sweaty clothing quickly
- Use breathable footwear
- Do not share personal items
- Treat all affected areas fully
