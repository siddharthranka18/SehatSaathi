Abdominal Pain - Triage Guidelines

OVERVIEW
Abdominal pain ranges from mild discomfort to severe emergency. Triage depends on severity, duration, location, and associated symptoms.

MILD ABDOMINAL PAIN (Home Care)
- Symptoms:
  * Mild cramping or soreness in abdomen
  * Duration: less than a few hours
  * Pain localized to one area
  * No fever
  * Able to eat and drink
  * Normal bowel movements
  * No vomiting or severe nausea

- Recommended actions:
  * Rest and avoid heavy foods
  * Drink fluids slowly (water, oral rehydration solution)
  * Apply warm pad or hot water bottle to abdomen
  * Monitor for worsening
  * Keep diet light (rice, banana, toast)

MODERATE ABDOMINAL PAIN (Visit PHC within 24-48 hours)
- Symptoms:
  * Moderate cramping or soreness lasting >3 hours
  * Pain with mild nausea or vomiting 1-2 times
  * Loose stools or slight diarrhea
  * Mild fever (99-100.5°F / 37.2-38°C)
  * Pain after eating specific foods
  * Feeling bloated or distended
  * Pain relieved by passing stool or gas

- Recommended actions:
  * Visit a healthcare worker if symptoms persist >24 hours
  * Avoid spicy, fatty, or greasy foods
  * Eat small frequent meals
  * Stay hydrated with ORS
  * Note foods that trigger pain

SEVERE ABDOMINAL PAIN (Seek immediate medical attention)
- Symptoms:
  * Severe, unbearable pain (sudden onset)
  * Rigid or extremely tender abdomen
  * Persistent vomiting (unable to keep fluids down)
  * Blood in stool or vomit
  * Fever >101°F (>38.3°C) with severe pain
  * Abdominal distention with pain and inability to pass stool/gas
  * Pain following recent trauma or injury
  * Pain in single location with severe tenderness
  * Associated with dizziness, weakness, or loss of consciousness
  * Yellow skin or eyes (jaundice)

- Emergency red flags:
  * Unbearable pain for >2 hours without relief
  * Severe vomiting with inability to retain fluids
  * Signs of shock (pale, cold, rapid heartbeat)
  * Severe tenderness to touch
  * Acute onset severe pain in elderly or with chronic conditions

SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS
- Pregnant women: any abdominal pain requires immediate medical evaluation
- Chronic conditions (ulcer, IBS, Crohn's): follow doctor's guidelines
- Medication history: some pain patterns suggest medication side effects
